KEEL

The keel goes down first. Everything else is built on it.

The operating model is rarely decided. It's assumed.

Nobody sits down and chooses whether one team builds everything, or business units build their own, or something between. It just happens, by default, by whoever moved first. Then eighteen months in, nobody can agree what the CoE was actually meant to be.

Undecided shows up as three failure modes

The centre that never lets go

Meant to be scaffolding while trust was built. A year later it's a permanent department, and teams tired of waiting build their own, unseen.

Governance nobody enforces

Federated only works if someone's actually reviewing the highest-risk work. Without that, it's not federated, it's ungoverned with a nicer name.

The model that hides the question

Hybrid looks like the safe middle option. Without an explicit rule for what goes where, it becomes the excuse never to decide.

This isn't about whether to use AI

It's about who builds it, who watches it, and whether that's been decided on purpose.

Three shapes exist. Most organisations are living inside one of them by accident. Keel exists to make that a choice instead.

01 · Fits high exposure, low capability

Centralised

One team builds and owns everything.

The real trade-off

Not speed versus control. Your first use case is slow and your tenth is fast, everything before it existed to build the infrastructure and trust that make case ten quick.

How it actually fails

Not chaos, the opposite. The centre was meant to be scaffolding and becomes a permanent department, while every team tired of waiting builds something on its own that nobody in the centre ever sees.

02 · Fits low exposure, real capability

Federated

Business units build their own. A light central function sets standards.

The real trade-off

Duplication for speed. Five teams quietly re-solving the same problem, because the cost of central coordination outweighs the redundancy.

How it actually fails

"Light touch" only works if the light touch is genuinely enforced. Otherwise it's not federated, it's ungoverned with a nicer name, and nobody finds out how exposed they are until something's already gone wrong.

03 · Fits mixed signals

Hybrid, centre-led

The centre builds the highest-exposure work. Business units build the rest, inside shared standards.

The trap

Everyone assumes this is the safe middle option. It's usually the hardest one to run well, and it's not a starting point, it's an endpoint you earn.

How it actually fails

Without an explicit, written rule for which use cases go where, it silently collapses: centralised for whatever's politically visible, ungoverned for everything else nobody's watching.

Organisations move between these three

They start centralised because capability is low and trust hasn't been earned. They move toward federation as a track record accumulates, but only if something is genuinely watching the whole time to prove that trust is warranted.

That's not a separate product next to Keel. It's what Soundings is for. It's the thing that makes moving between these three models safe, instead of a leap of faith.

A conversation, scored underneath, then written up properly

Not a form with three buttons per question. Claude asks, listens, and presses on a vague answer the same way it would with a colleague, "my CTO's aware of it" isn't the same as "confirmed in writing," and it won't move on until it knows which one is true.

Underneath, a fixed rule does the actual scoring, the same three factors from Keel's own operating model formula, so the recommendation means the same thing however you happened to answer that day.

Nine facts, one fixed formula

Three factors decide the operating model. Six more give the readiness picture underneath it.

Regulatory exposure

How exposed your likely AI use is: customer-facing, regulated sector, personal data at scale.

Existing capability

How much genuine in-house AI build capability already exists, not just one team.

Spend model

Concentrated in one place today, or already scattered across business units.

Score, then a rule

7 to 9: centralised. 5 to 6: hybrid. 3 to 4: federated. Stated plainly, not a hidden judgement call.
