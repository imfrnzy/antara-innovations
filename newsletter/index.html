Issue 2 · Shelf: Soundings

Your AI caught itself. That doesn't mean your controls worked.

An AI system got inside three real companies this year. Google's own account says the model stopped once it recognised it was operating inside real systems, not a test, and cited that as part of its explanation of the incident. Notice what's folded into that account: the system that got in is the same system that decided to stop. A system surviving its own mistake is evidence of recovery. It is not evidence that the control worked.

Issue 1 · Shelf: Sentinel

The people who have to do the pacing

Jacob Coxon posted his resignation from OpenAI and then Anthropic on a bench in Alamo Square, saying both companies are gambling with our lives. Days later, Dario Amodei published "We Must Pace the Frontier," proposing outside evaluators with employee-level access inside the labs. Banking has run this experiment for a century, resident examiners embedded alongside employees. The proposed AI evaluators can investigate and publish. Only a bank examiner can say no.

The idea

When machines become intelligent, we must awaken our own.

The most important questions about AI are no longer only technical. They are questions of judgement, trust, behaviour, leadership and responsibility.

What happens when a system can make decisions faster than the people supervising it? When automation changes what people notice? When confidence in an AI system becomes a substitute for understanding it? When the organisation cannot reconstruct why something happened?

The Awakened Intelligence looks beneath the AI headline to examine what is actually changing in people, in organisations and in the systems we are building around them.
