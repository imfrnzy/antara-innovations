Find out what your AI agents can actually do.

Every permission looks fine on its own. Someone connects an agent to the CRM, then the payment system, then customer email, and each new tool was one small, reasonable yes. Nobody's watching what all of it adds up to together, until the agent has already acted, not recommended, acted.

Sentinel isn't a network scan. It's not about what an agent is allowed to access, it's about what it can cause, whether that can be proven afterwards, and what changes once it's learned something.

A refund nobody reviewed

An agent can read and write to the CRM, the payment system and customer email. It can issue a refund and confirm it by email, entirely on its own, before anyone outside the team sees it.

A decision that already happened

An agent ranks candidates and auto-rejects anyone below a score. A wrongly rejected person has no record of why, and nobody outside the team reviewed the scoring logic.

The permission nobody remembers granting

A shared service account can reset passwords and unlock accounts, for anyone, based on ticket text alone. It's logged. The permission itself was never the problem, until it was.

Three different agents, the same gap underneath. Each permission was reasonable in isolation. Nobody had mapped what the combination could actually do.

What you'll come away with

  • Which agents actually exist
  • What each one is authorised to do
  • What it can see, and whether that source is trusted
  • What it can cause, at worst
  • Whether that can be reconstructed afterwards
  • Where consequence and traceability don't match

    A full Sentinel

    This assessment only knows what you know. The riskiest agent in most organisations is the one nobody's mentioned to you, built in a low-code tool, or paid for on a personal card. A full Sentinel starts with an identity sweep across every platform and service account, then interviews your teams directly under the same amnesty, and compares what it finds with what leadership knows.

    Sentinel gives a structured view based only on your answers, using a fixed set of rules: the same answers always give the same result. It is not legal advice and not a formal security certification. Where a fact was unknown, the map assumes the worse case and marks it provisional.
