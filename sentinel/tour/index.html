SENTINEL

Securing AI agents, from access to consequence.

Every permission looked fine on its own

An agent doesn't stay inside the job it was built for. Each new tool connected looked like one small, reasonable yes. By the time the combined effect is visible, it's already acted. Not recommended, acted.

It shows up as three kinds of gap

A refund nobody reviewed

An agent can read and write to the CRM, the payment system and customer email. It can issue a refund and confirm it, entirely on its own.

A decision that already happened

An agent auto-rejects candidates below a score. Nobody outside the team reviewed the logic that decided who gets a job.

The permission nobody remembers

A shared account can reset any password from ticket text alone. It's logged. The permission itself was the problem.

The question was never what it can access

It's what it can cause, what we can prove, and what we'll change.

Every individual permission can be reasonable. The danger only shows up once you look at what the agent can do with all of them at once, and by then it's already happened.

It starts with a promise, not a question

"We're taking stock of every AI agent actually running. Not to catch anyone out. If you've built or connected something that wasn't formally approved, that's fine, we need to know, and we won't treat telling us as a problem."

Sent by the sponsor, before a single conversation happens

Score by counting, not by feel

Fixed questions decide the band. Nobody scores by how the room feels that day.

Consequence: high

An irreversible action with no human step, sensitive data in view, or connected to more than one system at once.

Consequence: medium

Writes or updates a system of record, even if that write is reversible. "Reversible" is not the same as "safe."

Consequence: low

Read-only, or drafts something for a human to send.

Traceability

Three factual questions. Three yeses is Governed. One or two, Informal. Zero is Invisible.

Every answer gets chased, not just recorded

What's the single worst thing it could do right now, if it decided wrong?
Why we ask: a draft isn't a send. A read isn't a write. The control should scale with the consequence.
It could issue a refund and email the customer, before anyone else sees it.
Has anyone outside the team ever seen this agent do that?
