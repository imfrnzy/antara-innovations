Find out what AI is actually doing inside your organisation.

AI rarely turns up as a tidy, approved programme. A team has a problem and someone finds a tool. Someone else builds a shortcut because the approved system is too slow. A few months later AI is touching customer data, decisions and emails, and nobody holds the full picture.

Soundings builds that picture from what you tell it. You won't be asked to rate yourself out of ten. We work through what's running, what it touches, what happens when it's wrong, and who is watching.

A customer decision

An AI tool helps decide which customer complaints get escalated. The team calls it an internal productivity tool.

A workaround nobody sees

The approved AI platform goes down for a day. People switch to another tool that IT has never heard of, and don't switch back.

The human check

"Don't worry, a human checks everything." Nobody can show what was checked, when, or what the checker was meant to catch.

Three very different situations with the same gap underneath. Nobody can trace the path from what the AI produces to what it changes in the real world.

What you'll come away with

  • Which AI is actually in use
  • What it touches
  • What happens if it's wrong
  • Who knows it exists
  • How it's watched
  • Where the risk and the oversight don't match

    The detailed report

    The free map shows where each use sits. The detailed report shows why, and what to do about it.

    • Each use scored separately on regulation, data and reversibility
    • The evidence behind every classification, in your own words
    • Contradictions that came up during the interview
    • Facts still unproven, and who should prove them
    • An ordered action plan, with the review and approval checks each use needs
    • A one-page summary you can take to the board

    A full Soundings

    This assessment only knows what you know. The riskiest AI in most organisations is the use nobody has mentioned to you. A full Soundings interviews your teams directly, with an amnesty agreed upfront so people are honest, and compares their answers with yours. The gaps between the two are usually the finding.

    Soundings gives a structured view based only on your answers, using a fixed set of rules: the same answers always give the same result. It is not legal advice and not a formal regulatory classification. Where a fact was unknown, the map assumes the worse case and marks it provisional.
