Knowing what's running, and whether it's watched properly, before you build anything.

Nobody can answer a simple question

Ask five people in the same company whether AI is being used correctly, and you get five different answers. Nobody agreed what correct means.

It shows up as three kinds of gap

A customer decision

An AI tool helps decide which complaints get escalated. The team calls it an internal productivity tool.

A workaround nobody sees

The approved platform goes down for a day. People switch to a tool IT has never heard of, and don't switch back.

The human check

"Don't worry, a human checks everything." Nobody can show what was checked, or when.

The failure isn't the wrong AI

It's the wrong amount of oversight for what it actually does.

High-risk work with no one watching. Low-risk work buried under process so heavy that people build their own workaround. Both point the same direction: nobody set the level of scrutiny on purpose.

It starts with a promise, not a question

"We're taking stock of how AI is actually being used. Not to catch anyone out. If you're using a tool that wasn't formally approved, that's fine, we need to know, and we won't treat telling us as a problem."

Sent by the sponsor, before a single conversation happens

Every conversation covers the same four things

01

What's actually being used

Sanctioned or not. What they'd reach for if the approved tool were down.

02

What it touches

Internal drafting, or customer data, figures, a decision about a real person.

03

What happens if it's wrong

Checked before it goes anywhere, or does it go straight out.

04

Who knows about it

Manager, IT, risk, or nobody outside the room.

Score by counting, not by feel

Fixed questions decide the band. Nobody scores by how the room feels that day.

Regulatory exposure

High if it affects a named person's money, job, credit or a legal outcome, or reaches the public unreviewed.

Data sensitivity

High if it sees health data, financial or ID numbers, or credentials. Would it be a bad news story if it leaked.

Reversibility

High if it can't be recalled, an email sent, a decision communicated, money moved.

Oversight

Three factual questions. Three yeses is Governed. One or two, Informal. Zero is Invisible.

One question at a time, chased until it's answered

Does anyone check the score before it changes what happens to that customer?
Why we ask: if nobody's in between, a wrong score reaches the customer straight away.
Team leads spot check some. There's no log of it.
So a review may happen, but there's no record it does. Noted.

How it lands in the room matters as much as what's in it

The exposure zone gets named as a systems problem, not a team problem. The friction zone gets named just as loudly, since it's usually where the room agrees fastest. Every finding ends with a decision someone can act on by Monday, not a general call to take governance seriously.
