Every AI use raises the same question eventually

A supervisor, an auditor or a client asks who's accountable for a specific AI decision. The honest answer is usually "several people, informally", which in practice means no one.

One pass, three rulebooks

United Kingdom

SM&CR wants a named senior manager and evidence of reasonable steps. Consumer Duty wants the outcome, not just the intention.

Switzerland

FINMA's guidance wants governance, an inventory, and someone who can explain a result, not just produce one.

European Union

The AI Act wants classification, transparency, and specific duties where AI touches credit or life and health pricing.

Having it isn't the same as showing it

Is AI output checked by a person before it reaches a customer?
Yes, someone reviews it.
Why we ask twice: a belief and a record are different things.

Every answer earns a status, not a guess

The same fixed rule applies to everyone, so two people answering honestly land in the same place.

Ready

You have it, and you could show the evidence, on every question behind that obligation.

Partly

Some of it's there, or you have it but couldn't show it under questioning.

Not yet

It isn't in place. Not a verdict on you, a starting point for the work.

Can't tell yet

You didn't know. That's a finding too: if you can't tell, a supervisor won't either.

What it actually asks about

Only the questions that apply to you

Where you operate, your type of firm and where you actually use AI decide which of roughly twenty obligations get asked. A Swiss wealth manager sees far fewer than a bank licensed in all three places.
