Demonstration instruments

AI looks different in every organisation. Your assessment should too.

Tell us what you do, and we’ll focus on the risks, requirements and questions that matter to your organisation. No generic checklist. No irrelevant questions. Just a practical view of where you stand and what needs attention.

These instruments are demonstration versions. They show the method on information you give them and connect to nothing. The full capability is an agent deployed inside your organisation and configured to your regulator, delivered through an engagement.

Financial services

For banks, building societies, insurers, MGAs, brokers, private banks, wealth and asset managers, answering to the FCA, the PRA, FINMA or EU supervisors.

Financial services
AI regulatory readiness

Bearing

Readiness and the ability to demonstrate it are not the same thing.

The Bank of England and FCA's own survey found most firms name three or more people accountable for the same AI use. When a supervisor actually asks, that split is treated the same as no one being accountable at all.

Bearing tests what you could produce today against SM&CR, Consumer Duty, FINMA's guidance and the EU AI Act, not what you believe you know. Questions are graded on what you actually produce. Then a mock supervisor takes your strongest "yes" answers and asks for the actual document, and a re-run shows what moved since last time.

In full deployment: tests the evidence held in your own document stores against SM&CR, Consumer Duty, FINMA and the EU AI Act, configured to the regimes you answer to.

Financial services
Human judgement under pressure

Squall

Detection software catches some of it. Your team's judgement is what catches the rest.

Aviva alone reported uncovering a record £230m of bogus claims, with scammers using generative AI to fake crash photos, repair invoices and medical documents. The fake CFO message and the doctored claim photo are not hypothetical scenarios written for effect.

Six timed scenarios test whether your team would have caught it, scored for accuracy and for whether confidence matched reality. Wrong and certain is the pattern that actually costs money. Send your team a link and see where they differ from you, anonymously, once three have answered.

In full deployment: builds scenarios from your own claims and payments patterns and delivers them inside your existing training and email, tracked over time.

Agencies and media

For creative, media and marketing agencies working under the EU AI Act’s labelling rules, ASA guidance and clients’ AI clauses.

Agencies and media
AI content transparency

Ensign

A label doesn't rescue a claim that's misleading regardless.

Two thirds of big brands have internal AI-labelling rules, yet eight in ten want clearer guidance (WFA research, April 2026). The ASA says businesses cannot plead ignorance on AI in advertising.

Six scenarios test whether you know which rule actually applies. A seventh, written, question is graded on what you'd actually tell a client, not on how confident it sounds. Your team can take the same scenarios anonymously, so you see where the gap sits.

In full deployment: checks content and client clauses in your own workflow against the labelling rules of each market you work in.
